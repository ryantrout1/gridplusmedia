# How to Set Up a Google Business Profile

If you run a local business, your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees before they ever visit your website. It's the box that shows up on Google Search and Google Maps with your hours, address, phone number, photos and reviews. Setting it up correctly matters, because mismatched or missing details send customers to a locked door instead of your front counter.

Here's a straightforward walk through of the setup process, and what to watch for once it's live.

## Step 1: Create or claim your profile

Go to Google Business Profile Manager and search for your business name. If a profile already exists, perhaps created automatically from a past listing, you'll claim it instead of starting fresh. If nothing exists yet, you'll create a new one from scratch.

## Step 2: Enter your business name and category

Use the exact name your business operates under, the same name that appears on your website and your signage. Then choose the category that best matches what you do. This helps Google show your profile to the right searches, so take a moment to pick the most accurate option rather than the broadest one.

## Step 3: Add your location and service area

If customers visit you in person, add your street address exactly as it appears elsewhere, including suite numbers and spelling. If you travel to customers instead, like a plumber or mobile esthetician, you can set a service area instead of a public address.

## Step 4: Add contact details

Add a phone number and website link. Double check that the number matches the one on your website and any other listings. Small mismatches, like a missing extension or an old number, are a common reason customers can't reach a business.

## Step 5: Set your hours accurately

This step causes the most trouble. Your hours on Google need to match your actual hours and your website's hours exactly, including holiday closures and one-off changes. If you're closed for a staff training day or a special event, update it everywhere at once so nobody shows up expecting you to be open.

## Step 6: Verify your business

Google will ask you to verify that you're the real owner, usually by postcard, phone or email, depending on your business type. Verification can take a few days to a couple of weeks, so start this step early.

## Step 7: Add photos and a description

Upload clear photos of your storefront, interior and work, with alt text that describes what's shown so the images are accessible. Write a short description in your own words, covering what you offer and who you serve.

## Step 8: Turn on messaging and reviews

Decide whether you want to let customers message you directly through your profile. Set up alerts so you know when a new review comes in, since replying promptly and in your own voice matters more than the star rating alone.

## Keeping it accurate after setup
