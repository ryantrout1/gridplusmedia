# Make Your Name, Address and Phone Number Match Everywhere

Here is a simple rule: your business name, address and phone number should read the same everywhere. Your website, your Google profile, your Facebook page and every directory.

Small differences can confuse people, and they can confuse search. Is it Street or St? Suite or Ste? Pick one way to write it and use it every time.

## Pick your one version

Write down your business details exactly the way you want them to appear, in one place, like a note on your phone:

- Your business name, spelled and capitalized the way it is on your sign.
- Your street address, with the same short forms every time: Street or St, Suite or Ste. Choose one.
- Your phone number, in one format.
- Your website address.
- Your regular hours, and any holiday changes.

That note is your master copy. Everything else gets checked against it.

## The ten minute check

Take ten minutes, look at each place, and fix what does not match. Start with the places customers see most:

1. Your website, including the footer and the contact page.
2. Your Google Business Profile.
3. Your Facebook page.
4. Your Instagram bio.
5. Apple Maps, Bing Places and Yellow Pages, plus Foursquare, whose data feeds other apps.
6. Any other directory or booking site that lists you.

Look for the usual suspects. An old phone number. A suite number left off. A street spelled two ways. A name that changed when you rebranded. Hours that differ by an hour.

## Fix the biggest ones first

If you only have a few minutes, fix the places people see first: your Google profile and your website. Then work outward. You do not have to fix every directory in one sitting.

## Check again when anything changes

Moving, getting a new phone number, changing your name or your hours: each one means going back through the list. A reminder on your calendar every few months is enough to catch drift before customers do.

## Why it is worth ten minutes

When the details match, customers can find you, call you and get to your door without a surprise. When they do not, the mismatches add up, and sooner or later someone shows up at a locked door or gives up and calls somebody else.
