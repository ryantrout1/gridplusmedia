# What to Write on a Small Business Blog

Most owners know they should write on their blog. The ideas dry up after the first few articles, and sitting down to write is harder than it sounds. This guide gives you topics that come from your real work, and then a way to turn one into a finished article in about an hour.

## Part 1. Where topics come from

### Questions customers ask

Think about what people ask on the phone, at the counter or in messages before they book. Those are your best topics. If people ask what a job costs, how long it takes or what to expect on a first visit, write about that. You are writing down what you already say out loud.

### Your process

A walk through how you handle a job, step by step, helps a customer feel safe before they contact you. It is accurate because it describes what you already do.

### Seasons and timing

Local businesses have seasons. A home service company might write about getting ready for summer heat. A restaurant might write about a seasonal menu. These give you a reason to post that is not "we should post something."

### Each service, in plain words

Each main service can have an article that answers the basic questions a new customer would have. It gives you something to link to from posts and from your Google profile. See [How to Write a Service Page That Answers Your Customers](/guides/write-a-service-page-that-answers-your-customers/).

### Changes that matter to customers

Hours changes, new offerings, a new location. Short is fine, as long as it matches your Google profile and your website.

### How to choose

Someone comparing options wants to know what to look for and what to ask. A post that helps them decide, without overselling, builds trust.

## Part 2. Write one article in about an hour

Pick one question from the list above and follow these steps.

### Step 1. Write the question as the title

Use the customer's words. "How long does an AC tune up take?" beats "Our Maintenance Philosophy."

### Step 2. Write a two sentence answer first

Put the short answer at the top. People who only read the top still leave with something useful.

### Step 3. Add three to five points that explain it

Each point gets its own short heading and a few sentences. Use real examples from your work. Do not state numbers, prices or promises you cannot back up.

### Step 4. Say what to do next

End with one clear step: call, book or request a quote. Link to the service page that explains what you offer, and to your contact page.

### Step 5. Add one real photo

A phone photo of the work, the shop or the team, with permission. Write one plain sentence describing what it shows.

### Step 6. Check it against your facts

Every date, price, hour and claim should match your Google profile and website. If you are not sure, take it out.

## Use AI for a first draft

Start with the facts-first method in [How to Use AI to Write Your Marketing Without It Making Things Up](/guides/use-ai-to-write-your-marketing-without-it-making-things-up/), then use this:

> Write a short article for my small business website that answers this customer question: [the question]. Use only the facts in my facts sheet and the notes below. Do not invent prices, statistics, quotes or guarantees. Put a two sentence answer first, then three to five short sections, then one clear next step. Plain words, no exclamation points. If you need a fact that is not here, write [ASK ME].
>
> My notes on how I answer this: [two or three sentences in your own words]

Read the result against your facts before you publish it, and replace every [ASK ME].

## Check your work

- The title is a question a customer would actually ask.
- The short answer is at the top.
- Everything is true today and matches your other pages.
- There is a next step and a link to the related service page.
- You read it out loud and it sounds like you.

## Keep it up

One article a month is a good pace for most small businesses. A steady one beats a burst of five and then silence. Put the date on your calendar.
