# How to Ask for a Google Review Without Feeling Awkward

Asking for a review feels awkward. It gets easier with a few lines ready. This guide gives you the right moment, the exact words, and the one rule that matters most.

## Ask at the right time

Ask soon after the visit or the job, while it is fresh. A thank you, a smile, a finished job or a "that was great" are good moments. Waiting weeks means the moment has passed and the request feels random.

Make it a habit with every customer, not only the ones you expect to give five stars. Google's rules do not allow asking only your happiest customers, or discouraging people from leaving a negative review.

## Three lines you can say out loud

You do not need a speech. Here are the words:

- **Line one:** "I'm glad that worked out. Would you share that on Google? It helps a lot."
- **Line two:** "If you have a minute, I can text you the link right now."
- **Line three:** "Thank you. It means a lot to us."

Then send the text.

## The text

Keep it short, and put the link right in it:

> Thanks again today. Here is the link to leave a review: [your link]. We appreciate it.

To get your link, open your Google Business Profile and look for the option to ask for reviews, which gives you a link you can copy. Save it in your phone so you can paste it in a few seconds. The fewer steps between finishing with you and leaving a review, the more likely people are to follow through.

## One rule

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. No discount, no free item, no entry in a drawing. Just ask, and let the answer be honest. Google's rules do not allow rewards for reviews, and honest reviews are worth more to the next customer reading them anyway.

## Where to ask

- **At the counter or the door,** when someone thanks you.
- **At the end of a job,** before you pack up or drive away.
- **In a text or email afterward,** while the experience is fresh.
- **On a small card or a line on the receipt,** if you do not talk to customers face to face.

## After the review comes in

Reply. A short, warm reply tells the next reader that someone is paying attention, and it is how you thank the person who took the time.
