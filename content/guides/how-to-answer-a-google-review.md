# How to Answer a Google Review, Good or Bad

When someone leaves a review, you are not only talking to them. The next customer reads your reply too. Someone who has never visited is deciding whether to walk in, and your reply is their first sign of how you treat people.

So keep it simple. This guide gives you a three line formula, a way to handle a bad review, and a routine that takes about ten minutes a week.

**You need:** a verified Google Business Profile.

## Where to reply

Sign in at business.google.com, open your profile, and look for **Read reviews**. Find the review you want to answer and use the reply option on it. Menus change now and then, so look for the closest match. Owners and managers on the profile can both reply.

## The three line reply

- **Line one:** thank them.
- **Line two:** name one real detail from their visit.
- **Line three:** invite them back.

Here is an example for a made-up review about a friendly front desk:

> Thank you for the kind words. We are glad you felt so welcome at the front desk. We would love to see you again.

That works for kind reviews. It also works as a calm starting point for a tough one.

A few rules keep it honest:

- Do not copy and paste the same reply every time. A single true detail shows you were paying attention.
- Only mention what really happened. If you do not remember, keep it general.
- Leave out private details, like what someone ordered, their health or their address.
- Do not use a reply to offer a deal or promotion. Google advises against it.

Google's own advice is to keep replies professional, polite and brief, and to reply promptly.

## Should I answer a bad review?

Yes. A bad review stings, and the urge is to argue. Resist it. Answer once, calmly, in public.

1. Say you are sorry they had a poor experience.
2. Do not argue or explain who was right.
3. Offer to make it right offline, with a phone number or an email they can use. Only offer what you will really do.
4. Keep the reply short.

Here is another made-up example:

> We are sorry your visit did not go the way it should have. That is not what we want for anyone who comes in. Please call us at the number on our profile so we can make it right.

Remember who you are really writing for: the next person reading. They want to see how you treat people when things go wrong. A calm answer to a hard review often tells them more than a glowing one.

If you did make a mistake, say so plainly. If the problem was complicated, ask the reviewer to contact you privately. Do not make personal attacks or share private information.

## What if the review is unfair or fake?

If a review breaks Google's rules, for example spam, or a review from someone who never visited, you can report it. In the meantime, reply calmly, and never answer in kind. Do not write reviews yourself, or ask friends to write them, to push a bad one down. Fake reviews put your listing at risk.

## Use AI for a first draft, carefully

AI can save time on replies, but it can also add things that did not happen. Use this and keep the reply to what the review says:

> Here is a Google review of my business: [paste the review]. Write a reply in three lines: thank them, mention one real detail from their review, and invite them back. Use only what is in the review. Do not mention any service, price, name or event that is not in the review. Do not offer discounts. Plain words, no exclamation points. If it is a negative review, apologize without arguing and offer to talk by phone.

## Keep up with them

A reply that goes out a week late still helps, so there is no need to feel behind. Once or twice a week, set aside ten minutes and catch up on any you missed. To get more reviews coming in, see [How to Ask for a Google Review Without Feeling Awkward](/guides/ask-for-reviews-without-feeling-awkward/).

## Check your work

- Every review has a reply.
- Each reply names one real detail.
- None of them offers a deal or shares private information.
- Bad reviews got a calm answer and a way to reach you.
