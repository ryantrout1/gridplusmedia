# How to Get Found on Google as a Local Business

If customers search for what you do and your business doesn't show up, or shows up with the wrong hours, you're losing people before they ever call. Getting found on Google isn't one trick. It's a handful of things working together, kept accurate and kept current.

## Start With a Complete, Accurate Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is usually the first thing a nearby customer sees. It needs your correct hours, address, phone number and category, and it needs to stay that way. If your profile says one thing and your website says another, Google notices the mismatch and so do customers who show up expecting something different from what they find.

The fix is simple to describe and tedious to keep up with on your own: whenever something changes, like a holiday closure or new hours, update it everywhere at once.

## Keep Your Details Matching Everywhere

Google isn't the only place people check. Your website, Instagram, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Foursquare, Yelp and other directories all carry your business details too. If even one of them is out of date, you risk a customer showing up to a locked door.

Listing consistency across all these places is one of the clearest signals that tells Google your business is legitimate and active, and it's one of the easiest things to let slip when you're busy running the business itself.

## Post Regularly, Not Just Once in a While

A Google profile or Instagram account that hasn't posted in months reads as inactive, to both customers and to Google. Regular posts, whether on your Google profile, Instagram or Facebook, show that your business is open, active and worth a second look.

The content doesn't need to be complicated. It just needs to start from what's actually true about your business, so it sounds like you and stays accurate.

## Give People Somewhere to Land

A post that catches someone's eye is only half the job. From there, people should land somewhere that tells them more: a blog article on your own site that Google can also find, which then points to the specific service page that explains what you offer. That clear path, from post to article to service page to booking, is what turns a glance into an actual customer.

## Reply to Reviews and Comments

Reviews affect how your business ranks locally and how people decide whether to choose you. Replying to every review, in your own voice, shows you're paying attention. It also keeps your tone consistent across Google, Yelp and Facebook, rather than silence in some places and responses in others.

## Keep It Going Over Time

Getting found on Google isn't a one-time setup. It's hours and details that stay matching when things change, posts that keep going out on schedule, and reviews that get replied to every time, not just when there's time. A 90-day plan works through this in cycles: a setup call to learn the business, building the website, Google profile and social accounts, running the plan week to week, then checking what worked and planning the next quarter.
