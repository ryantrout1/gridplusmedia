# How to Get Found on Google as a Local Business

Getting found on Google is not one trick. It is a handful of ordinary things, done in the right order and kept up. Nobody can promise you a spot at the top, and Google says there are no secrets that put a site first automatically. What you can do is make it easy for Google and for customers to see what you do, where you are and that you are open.

This guide is the order of work. Reading it takes about 10 minutes, and each step links to a detailed guide with its own time.

## Step 1. See what customers see today

Search your business name and city on your phone, and look at what shows up. Write down what is wrong. See [Check How Your Business Shows Up on Google, and Fix What Is Wrong](/guides/check-how-your-business-shows-up-on-google-and-fix-it/).

## Step 2. Get your Google profile in place

This is the most important step for a local business. Claim it, verify it, and fill in every section: category, services, service area or address, hours, description and photos.

- [How to Claim and Verify Your Google Business Profile](/guides/claim-and-verify-your-google-business-profile/)
- [How to Choose Your Google Categories, Services and Service Area](/guides/choose-your-google-categories-services-and-service-area/)
- [How to Write Your Google Business Description](/guides/write-your-google-business-description/)
- [Which Photos to Put on Your Google Listing](/guides/photos-that-get-you-found-on-google/)

## Step 3. Make your details match everywhere

Your name, address, phone number and hours should read the same on your website, your Google profile, Facebook, Instagram and the directories customers use, such as Apple Maps, Bing Places and Yellow Pages. See [Make Your Name, Address and Phone Number Match Everywhere](/guides/make-your-name-address-and-phone-match/), and [Set Up Facebook and Instagram for Your Business](/guides/set-up-facebook-and-instagram-for-your-business/).

## Step 4. Get honest reviews and reply to them

Reviews affect how people decide whether to choose you, and Google lists them as part of how it judges how well known a business is. Ask every customer, make the link easy, and reply to what comes in.

- [How to Ask for a Google Review Without Feeling Awkward](/guides/ask-for-reviews-without-feeling-awkward/)
- [How to Answer a Google Review, Good or Bad](/guides/how-to-answer-a-google-review/)

## Step 5. Give people a page that answers their question

A profile gets someone's attention. Your website has to give them the details. Write one page for each main service, plus an article now and then that answers a question customers ask. See [How to Write a Service Page That Answers Your Customers](/guides/write-a-service-page-that-answers-your-customers/) and [What to Write on a Small Business Blog](/guides/small-business-blog-topics/). A simple path is: a post catches their eye, an article answers a question, and a service page explains what you offer and how to book.

## Step 6. Post on a schedule you can keep

An account that has not posted in months can look closed. You do not need to post daily. You need a pace you can hold. See [A 90 Day Posting Plan You Can Copy](/guides/90-day-posting-plan-you-can-copy/) and [What to Post on Your Google Business Profile](/guides/what-to-post-on-your-google-profile/).

## Step 7. Check, change one thing, repeat

Every few weeks look at your calls, messages and bookings, and change one thing at a time. See [How to Tell If a Change to Your Marketing Worked](/guides/how-to-tell-if-a-marketing-change-worked/).
