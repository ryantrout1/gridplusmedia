# Marketing a Restaurant in the West Valley

When someone in the West Valley decides where to eat, many of them look at Google first. They look at your hours, a few photos and your reviews, then they get in the car. So the small things on your Google profile do real work. This guide covers the ones worth doing first.

## Start with your hours

People act on the hours shown on Google. If Google says open and your door says closed, that is a bad moment for everyone, and it is the kind of thing that turns into a bad review.

Today, compare your hours on Google to the sign on your door. Open your Google Business Profile, choose Edit profile, then Hours, and fix anything that is off. Then look at the same hours on your website, your Facebook page, your Instagram bio and any directory that lists you, and make them all say the same thing.

## Set special hours before any holiday

Do this before the holiday, not after someone has shown up. Add special hours in your profile for each day you open late, close early or close for the day. It takes a few minutes. Then post the same hours on Facebook and Instagram, so a regular who never opens Google still sees them.

## Make it easy to leave a review

Put a small card on each table, or a line on the check, that asks guests to leave a Google review. Make the path easy, like a code that opens your review page. Keep the words short: "Thanks for dining with us. A Google review helps neighbors find us."

Do not offer anything in return, such as a discount or a free dessert. Google's rules do not allow rewards for reviews, and an honest review means more to the next hungry reader anyway. Just ask kindly. Then reply to what comes in. A short, warm reply tells the next reader that someone is paying attention.

## Show the real place

Skip the stock photos. People want to see the room they are about to walk into and the food they are about to order. Take photos on your phone in normal light: the dining room, the specials board, a plate as it really looks, the people who work there. Add a few at a time, and replace anything that no longer looks like your place.

## Plan for the slow weeks

Every restaurant has a slow stretch. For some it is January, after the holidays. In the West Valley it can also be summer, when snowbirds head home and the heat keeps people in. Plan for yours now, before you feel it. Pick a few dishes, or a few regulars' favorites, and plan posts around them. Show the kitchen, the specials board and the people who work there. Give folks a reason to come in that is not a deal: a new menu item, a cozy booth, a warm bowl on a cool evening.

Then write the posts ahead of time, while you are not stressed. A week of posts written on a calm afternoon beats a week of posts typed between orders.

## A five minute check each week

- Are your hours right on Google, your website and Facebook?
- Are special hours set for any holiday coming up?
- Did you add one new real photo?
- Have you replied to the newest reviews?
- Is one post scheduled for the week ahead?
