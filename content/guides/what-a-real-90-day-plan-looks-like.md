# What a Real 90 Day Plan Looks Like, With a Sample

A 90 day plan is not a mystery. It is four steps you repeat: plan, do, check and adjust. This guide shows what each step looks like, why we plan a quarter at a time, and a labeled sample you can copy. The sample is for an imaginary business. It shows a method, not a result.

## Why a quarter, not a month

Changes in search can take weeks or months to show, so one month rarely gives you enough to judge. A month is also long enough to stay stuck on something that is not working. A quarter gives you room to try something, look at what happened and adjust. We also like that it lines up with the seasons many local businesses live by.

So how long before marketing works? Nobody can give you an exact number, but about 90 days is a fair window to judge a plan. Search can take a while to notice a business that has changed, and people often need to see you more than once before they call. Give a plan a full quarter before you judge it.

## The four steps

### Step 1. Plan

Pick three things to say this quarter, and choose a pace you can hold, even in a busy week. For a simple pattern to copy, see [A 90 Day Posting Plan You Can Copy](/guides/90-day-posting-plan-you-can-copy/).

### Step 2. Do

Write the posts before you post any of them, then post on schedule. Posting becomes routine instead of a scramble.

### Step 3. Check

Four weeks into a plan, it is tempting to stare at every number you can find. Do not. Look at three things only:

1. Did the posts go out on time? Count the misses, and do not judge.
2. Which post got the most response? Note what it was about.
3. Did anyone call, click or send a message? That is what the posts are for.

Write the answers on a sheet of paper. If the posts went out and someone reached out, the plan is working well enough to keep going. If not, you know where to look next. No dashboards needed.

### Step 4. Adjust

Change one or two things at a time, and keep everything else the same. Say you change your posting days, your photos, your wording, your hours and your hashtags all in one week. Then things get better or worse. Which change did it? You will never know. Look again in three weeks, then decide: keep it, drop it, or try the next thing. It feels slow, but it is how you actually learn what works.

## A sample plan

**SAMPLE ONLY. An imaginary neighborhood shop. No real client, no results.**

### Month one: plan and start

- **Plan:** choose three themes, such as a tip, a look behind the scenes and a customer question. Choose a pace.
- **Week one, a kickoff week of short posts:**
  - Monday: meet the owner. A friendly photo and two sentences about what you do.
  - Tuesday: a photo of something you made or fixed, with one line on how.
  - Wednesday: answer a question customers ask all the time.
  - Thursday: a behind the scenes shot of your day. Keep it simple.
  - Friday: remind people of your hours and say thank you.
- **Weeks two to four:** one post a week, rotating the three themes.
- **End of week four:** the four week check. Three questions, written on paper.

### Month two: keep what worked, change one or two things

- Repeat the theme that got the most response.
- Change one or two things, such as the posting day or the kind of photo. Leave the rest alone.
- Look again in three weeks.

### Month three: look back and plan the next quarter

- Keep going at the same pace.
- Around week twelve, look back over the whole quarter. What went out? What got a response? Did anyone call or click?
- Write down what to keep, what to drop and one thing to try.
- Plan the next quarter, then start the cycle again.

Small, steady posts are the point. The plan is built so that the quarter is already written down before the busy weeks arrive.
