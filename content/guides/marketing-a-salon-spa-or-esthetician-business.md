# Marketing a Salon, Spa or Esthetician Business

Beauty businesses run on trust and repeat visits. Most of what helps online is small and can be done between clients. Pick the right categories on Google. Share photos of your work with permission. Book the next visit before someone leaves the chair.

## Pick the right Google categories

Here is one fix worth ten minutes today. Open your Google profile and look at your main category. Pick the one that most accurately describes what you do. If other categories truly describe your services, add them, but keep the list short.

Why it matters: the category tells Google what kind of business you are. A wrong one can send you to the wrong searches, and the right clients never see you. Be honest and specific. Do not add categories that do not fit. Check it, fix it, move on.

## Show the work, with permission

For salons, spas and estheticians, your best marketing is the work itself. A before and after, or a finished look, shows people what to expect.

One rule: ask the client first. Some prefer no face in the photo, and that is fine. Keep the ask simple: "May I share a photo of this look on our page?" Then respect the answer. A photo of hair from behind, a close up of the finished look or a tidy treatment room can show your skill without showing the person.

## Ask clients to rebook

Before your client leaves the chair, offer the next appointment. Pull up the calendar and ask, "Want to book your next visit while you're here?" It is easier to say yes when the calendar is right in front of them.

## Fill the slow days

Look at your slow days. If a Tuesday has open spots, post them that morning on Facebook and Instagram. Keep it plain: the day, the time, and how to book.

## Keep your details matching

Clients check Google, Facebook, Instagram and your booking page before they book. If your hours say one thing on Google and another on your booking page, someone shows up at a locked door. When you change your hours or take a day off, change them everywhere the same day.
